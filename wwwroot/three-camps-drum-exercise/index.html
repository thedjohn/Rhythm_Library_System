What Is Three Camps?

Three Camps is a traditional rudimental snare drum exercise used in drum education for generations. In the Rhythm Library System, it becomes a rhythmic framework — a sequence of note values that you apply different sticking patterns to. Each time you change the sticking pattern, you hear a completely different musical phrase built from the same underlying rhythm.

How to Practice Three Camps

  1. Start slow with a click track — Begin at 60–70 BPM. Focus on accuracy before speed.
  2. Choose one sticking pattern — Select a single pattern from the GK 16ths group.
  3. Loop a single measure — Isolate difficult measures until they feel natural.
  4. Play through the full sequence — Play from beginning to end without stopping.
  5. Increase tempo gradually — Move up 5 BPM only when the pattern feels relaxed.
  6. Move it around the kit — Apply patterns to toms, cymbals, and kick.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Three Camps suitable for beginners?

Yes. Starting at a slow tempo with simple sticking patterns makes Three Camps accessible from the very beginning. The system scales with your skill level — beginners start with alternating strokes while advanced players work through the full pattern library.

How long should I practice Three Camps each session?

15 to 20 minutes per session is enough to make consistent progress. Focused, slow practice beats long unfocused sessions every time.

Do I need the book to use the Three Camps app?

No. The free interactive app works on its own as a practice tool. The 83-page PDF book by Gordy Knudtson provides the full system with the complete pattern library and all rhythm sequences.

What is the connection between Three Camps and the Syncopation book?

The Rhythm Library System concept is inspired by the practice of applying sticking patterns to the solo pages in Ted Reed's Syncopation. Three Camps is one of the rhythmic frameworks used in this approach.
