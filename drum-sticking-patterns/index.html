Why Sticking Patterns Matter

A sticking pattern determines which hand plays each note in a phrase. Change the sticking and the same rhythm sounds completely different. The Rhythm Library System uses a library of sticking patterns applied to the same rhythmic sequences — you get hundreds of musical ideas from a compact, structured practice system.

Core Pattern Types

  • Alternating Sticking — Right-left-right-left. The starting point for all sticking development.
  • Right-Hand Lead — Develops natural right-hand dominance for leading phrases.
  • Left-Hand Lead — Essential for ambidexterity and kit placement.
  • Double Stroke Patterns — Two strokes per hand. Builds rebound control for faster tempos.
  • Paradiddle-Based Patterns — Single-single-double combinations with natural accent points.
  • Accent-Driven Patterns — Designed around accent placement for dynamic control.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are drum sticking patterns?

Drum sticking patterns are sequences that determine which hand plays each note in a rhythmic phrase. Common patterns include alternating strokes (RLRL), paradiddles (RLRR LRLL), double strokes (RRLL), and accent-driven combinations. Practicing sticking patterns builds hand control, speed, and creative drumset vocabulary.

What is a paradiddle?

A paradiddle is a sticking pattern that follows the sequence RLRR LRLL — single-single-double repeated on each hand. It is one of the most fundamental rudiments in drumming and forms the basis for many fill and solo ideas when applied around the drumset.

How long should I practice sticking patterns each day?

15 to 20 minutes of focused daily practice is enough to make consistent progress. The key is slow, intentional practice — choosing one pattern, starting at 60 BPM, and only increasing tempo when the pattern is even and relaxed.

What is the best sticking pattern for beginners?

Alternating sticking (RLRL) is the best starting point for beginners. It builds even hand balance and consistent tone before moving on to more complex patterns like paradiddles and double strokes.
